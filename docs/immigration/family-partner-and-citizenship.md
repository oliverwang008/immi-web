> **Last researched: 2026-06-29. Verify figures against official sources before relying on them.**

This is a reference for the "AussieVisa Tracker" project covering current (FY2025–26) Australian
family, partner, and citizenship pathways, with emphasis on 2024–2026 changes.

## 1. Partner visas

Australia's partner visa system is built around a **two-stage, temporary → permanent** structure.
In almost all cases you apply for the temporary and permanent stages **together in a single
application** and pay **one combined Visa Application Charge (VAC)**. The permanent stage is
normally decided around **two years after the original application date**, once the relationship is
re-assessed as genuine and continuing.

| Stage | Onshore (in Australia) | Offshore (outside Australia) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Temporary | **Subclass 820** | **Subclass 309** |
| Permanent | **Subclass 801** | **Subclass 100** |

Plus the **Subclass 300 Prospective Marriage ("fiancé(e)") visa** — a temporary offshore visa for
those intending to marry, which then leads into an onshore 820/801 partner application after marriage.

### 1.1 Eligibility (high level)

- Applicant must be the **spouse** (married) or **de facto partner** of an Australian citizen,
  Australian permanent resident, or eligible New Zealand citizen.
- The relationship must be **genuine and continuing**, with the couple living together (or not
  living separately on a permanent basis).
- **De facto** relationships generally require **12 months of cohabitation** before lodgement
  (unless the relationship is registered in a relevant Australian state/territory, or compelling
  circumstances apply).
- A **sponsor** (the Australian partner) must be approved; sponsorship is assessed alongside the
  visa.
- Standard **health** and **character** requirements apply, including police clearances.
- **Subclass 300 (Prospective Marriage):** the couple must have **met in person**, be known to each
  other, and **intend to marry** within the visa validity period (the 300 is granted for a fixed
  period within which the marriage must occur).

### 1.2 Two-stage structure

1. **Temporary grant (820 or 309):** lets the applicant live, work and study in Australia while the
   permanent application is assessed.
2. **Permanent grant (801 or 100):** generally eligible to be decided ~2 years after the initial
   lodgement date. Long-term relationships, or those with children/family violence considerations,
   may be eligible for the permanent stage sooner / concurrently.
3. **Subclass 300 path:** 300 granted offshore → marry within validity → lodge onshore **820/801**
   partner application (a reduced subsequent VAC typically applies for the 820 after holding a 300 —
   *confirm current amount; see flagged items*).

### 1.3 Visa Application Charge (base, FY2025–26)

| Visa | Base VAC (primary applicant) | Notes |
|------|------------------------------|-------|
| Partner 820/801 (onshore, combined) | **~AUD 9,365** ⚠️ | Single combined charge covers both temporary and permanent stages. |
| Partner 309/100 (offshore, combined) | **~AUD 9,365** ⚠️ | Same base VAC as onshore. |
| Prospective Marriage 300 | **~AUD 9,365** ⚠️ | Secondary sources vary (some quote ~8,850–9,095); reconfirm. |

⚠️ = not confirmed against a primary Home Affairs page during this pass (403/JS-rendered). The
~AUD 9,365 figure is consistently reported by multiple migration-agent/law-firm sources for the
2025–26 year. Additional applicant charges apply for dependants (different rates for under-18 vs
18+). The **Subclass 820 subsequent VAC for a 300-visa holder** is lower — confirm current amount.

### 1.4 Indicative processing times

> Home Affairs publishes "50% / 90%" global processing percentiles that move month to month.
> Figures below reflect reporting around **September–October 2025**. Reconfirm on the live
> Home Affairs "Visa processing times" tool.

| Visa | 50% of applications within | 90% of applications within |
|------|---------------------------|-----------------------------|
| 820 (onshore temporary) | ~20 months ⚠️ | ~26 months ⚠️ |
| 801 (onshore permanent stage) | ~7 months ⚠️ | ~13 months ⚠️ |
| 309 (offshore temporary) | ~16 months ⚠️ | ~23 months ⚠️ |
| 100 (offshore permanent stage) | ~7 months ⚠️ | ~13 months ⚠️ |
| 300 (Prospective Marriage) | — | ~23 months ⚠️ |

⚠️ Cross-checked against secondary sources only. General planning ranges quoted: onshore 820/801
~9–26 months, offshore 309/100 ~9–24 months, 300 ~15–28 months. **Front-loading** evidence
(health checks, police clearances, relationship evidence) at lodgement reduces delays.

### 1.5 Sponsorship limits

- **Lifetime limit of two (2) partner sponsorships**, counting any combination of subclasses 820,
  801, 309, 100 and 300.
- A **minimum 5-year gap** between sponsorships. The 5 years runs **from the date the earlier
  partner application was lodged** (not from grant).
- Limits count **regardless of outcome** — a refused or withdrawn application still counts, and a
  300 that did not result in marriage still counts.
- The Department **may waive** these limits in compelling/compassionate circumstances (e.g. death
  of a former partner, children of the current relationship, family violence) where refusing would
  cause undue hardship in a genuine relationship.

## 2. Parent visas

Parent visas split into two tracks with **dramatically** different costs and wait times:

- **Contributory** (pay a much larger second VAC in exchange for *relatively* faster processing):
  permanent **143** and temporary **173** (and their onshore equivalents 864 / 884).
- **Non-contributory** ("ordinary") **Parent 103** (and onshore Aged Parent 804): far cheaper but
  with **multi-decade** queues.

All parent visas require meeting the **balance-of-family test** (at least half the applicant's
children live permanently in Australia, or more children in Australia than any other single
country) and, for contributory visas, an **Assurance of Support (AoS)** bond.

### 2.1 Comparison

| | Contributory Parent 143 (permanent) | Contributory Parent 173 (temporary, 2-stage) | Non-contributory Parent 103 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Permanent | Temporary (2 years), then pay balance to move to 143 | Permanent |
| Approx. total VAC | First instalment ~**4,770** + second instalment ~**43,600–43,900 per adult** ⚠️ (~**48,495 total** commonly quoted) | Split across 173 then 143 (≈ similar total when combined) | ~**5,125** (first) + smaller second instalment ⚠️ |
| Assurance of Support | **Required** (10-year bond via Services Australia; commonly cited ~**14,000** couple / ~**10,000** single — confirm) ⚠️ | Required | Required |
| Indicative new-application wait | **~15 years** ⚠️ | similar contributory queue | **~29–33 years** ⚠️ |
| Best for | Families who can fund faster reunion | Spreading cost over time | Those who cannot afford contributory cost and accept very long wait |

⚠️ Dollar amounts and wait estimates from secondary sources; not confirmed against the live
Home Affairs 143/103 pages this pass.

### 2.2 Queues, caps and planning

- The **whole Parent program is capped** (~**8,500** places per year), with the large majority
  (~6,800) reserved for **contributory** subclasses and only a small share (~1,700) for
  **non-contributory** parent visas. Because demand vastly exceeds places, applications that pass
  initial checks are placed in a **queue** and released for final processing by **queue date**.
- **Queue release (reported as at ~31 July 2025):**
  - **Contributory parent (143/173):** applications with a queue date up to around **May 2018**
    released for final processing. ⚠️
  - **Non-contributory parent (103/804):** applications with a queue date up to around
    **January 2013** released. ⚠️
- **Home Affairs' own published estimates for *new* applications** (as reported): around
  **15 years** for contributory, and around **29–33 years** for non-contributory. ⚠️ These are not
  legally binding processing-time commitments — they are demand-based estimates.

> **Developer note:** Because parent visas are queue-based rather than ordinary "processing time"
> based, the tracker should model them on **queue date + queue release date** rather than a simple
> percentile, and should surface the prominent "these waits are measured in *years/decades*" warning.

## 3. Child visas

For dependent children of an Australian citizen, permanent resident, or eligible NZ citizen.

| Visa | Subclass 101 | Subclass 802 |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Where applied | **Offshore** (child outside Australia) | **Onshore** (child in Australia on a valid visa) |
| Type | Permanent | Permanent |
| For | Dependent child / adopted child of the sponsor | Same |

- **Eligibility (high level):** child must be the sponsor's biological, adopted, or step-child; be
  **dependent**; and generally be **under 18**, OR aged 18–24 and a **full-time student financially
  dependent** on the sponsor, OR 18+ with an incapacity for work due to disability. Must be
  unmarried / not in a de facto relationship.
- **Sponsor obligations:** the parent sponsors and assumes responsibility, including providing
  accommodation and financial support (commonly described as covering the child's costs for the
  first ~2 years).
- **Base VAC:** approximately **AUD 3,235** for the main applicant (2025), plus additional charges
  for dependants. ⚠️ (confirm on Home Affairs 101/802 pages.)
- Child visas are **demand-driven** within the family stream (not subject to the harsh parent/other-
  family queues), so processing is generally far faster than parent or "other family" visas.

## 4. Other family visas

These are **capped and queued**, demand far exceeds places, and waits are extreme. Each has an
**offshore** and an **onshore** subclass:

| Category | Offshore | Onshore | Who it's for |
|----------|----------|---------|--------------|
| Aged Dependent Relative | **114** | **838** | Older, single relative wholly dependent on an Australian relative |
| Remaining Relative | **115** | **835** | Person whose only near relatives live in Australia |
| Carer | **116** | **836** | Person coming to care for an Australian relative with a long-term medical condition |

**Indicative status (as reported, ~2025):** ⚠️

- **Carer (116/836):** queue release around applications with queue date up to **31 December 2023**;
  new queued applications estimated **~4.5 years** to be released for final processing.
- **Remaining Relative (115/835)** and **Aged Dependent Relative (114/838):** queue release around
  applications with queue date up to **30 June 2013**; new queued applications estimated
  **~50 years** to be released. (Yes — decades; effectively impractical for most new applicants.)

> **Developer note:** As with parent visas, model these on queue date / queue release date and show
> a strong warning about multi-decade waits for Remaining Relative and Aged Dependent Relative.

## 5. Australian citizenship by conferral

The standard pathway for permanent residents (and the typical endpoint after a skilled visa →
permanent residency journey).

### 5.1 General residence requirement

To apply for citizenship by conferral under the **general residence requirement**, an applicant
must, immediately before applying:

- Have **lawfully lived in Australia for 4 years**, AND
- Have held a **permanent visa (or Special Category subclass 444 visa) for the last 12 months**,
  AND
- Have **not been absent from Australia for more than 12 months total** during the 4-year period,
  AND **not more than 90 days** in the **12 months immediately before applying**.

> This is the commonly cited **"4 years lawful residence including 12 months as a permanent
> resident"** rule. Time in Australia on temporary visas (e.g. student, skilled-provisional, bridging)
> counts toward the 4 years **provided** the final 12 months are as a PR/SCV holder. Use the
> Home Affairs **Residence Calculator** to confirm eligibility dates.

Additional requirements: be a **permanent resident** at time of application **and** decision; meet
the **good character** requirement; intend to reside in / maintain a close link with Australia; and
(for applicants generally aged **18–59**) **pass the citizenship test**.

### 5.2 The citizenship test

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Format | **20 multiple-choice questions** |
| Source material | *Australian Citizenship: Our Common Bond* booklet |
| Overall pass mark | **≥ 75%** (i.e. **15 of 20** correct) |
| Australian Values questions | Includes **5 Australian Values questions** — **all 5 must be answered correctly** or you fail regardless of overall score |
| Who must sit it | Generally applicants aged **18–59** |

### 5.3 Processing times & ceremony

- Citizenship-by-conferral application processing: commonly reported around **90% within ~8–12
  months** ⚠️ (Home Affairs publishes a moving benchmark; reconfirm).
- After approval, you must attend a **citizenship ceremony** and make the **Australian Citizenship
  Pledge** — reported as **90% of approved applicants offered a ceremony within ~6 months** of
  approval. ⚠️ Citizenship takes legal effect at the ceremony.

### 5.4 General PR → citizenship pathway (from skilled visas)

A typical end-to-end journey the tracker should be able to model:

1. **Temporary / provisional skilled visa** (e.g. subclass 482, 485, 491) — time in Australia here
   counts toward the 4-year residence requirement.
2. **Permanent residency** (e.g. subclass 189 / 190 / 186, or transition from 491 → 191) — the
   **last 12 months before applying** for citizenship must be held as a **PR** (or SCV).
3. **Citizenship eligibility:** once 4 years' lawful residence + 12 months as PR + absence limits +
   good character + test are satisfied.
4. **Apply → test/interview → decision → ceremony → citizen.**

> **Developer note:** The two binding levers for the tracker's citizenship eligibility calculator
> are (a) total lawful residence ≥ 4 years, and (b) ≥ 12 months as PR immediately before applying,
> subject to the absence caps (≤ 12 months total over 4 years; ≤ 90 days in final 12 months).

## 6. Migration Program planning context (FY2025–26)

| Item | FY2025–26 |
|------|-----------|
| Total permanent Migration Program | **185,000 places** (unchanged from 2024–25) |
| Family stream | **~52,500 places** (~28% of program) |
| Partner visas (indicative planning level) | **~40,500 places** — Partner & Child remain **demand-driven** |
| Parent visas | Capped at **~8,500** places (majority contributory) |

Partner and Child visas are **demand-driven** (planning numbers are indicative, not hard caps),
whereas Parent and Other Family visas are **capped and queued**. This distinction is the single most
important modelling difference for the tracker's wait-time logic.
