# Australian Skilled Migration — Points Test & EOI / SkillSelect Reference

> Last researched: 2026-06-28. Verify figures against official sources before relying on them.

This is a developer reference for **AussieVisa Tracker**, covering the points test for
subclasses **189 / 190 / 491**, the minimum threshold and real-world cut-offs, the
EOI / SkillSelect mechanics, and upcoming reforms. Australian English is used throughout.

Anything time-sensitive carries an **"as of"** note. Where a figure could not be confirmed
directly from a primary (homeaffairs.gov.au) source, it is explicitly flagged as **UNVERIFIED**.

> **Scope note on the three visas**
> - **Subclass 189 (Skilled Independent, points-tested):** permanent, no sponsor. Adds **0** visa-type points.
> - **Subclass 190 (Skilled Nominated):** permanent, requires State/Territory nomination. Adds **+5** points.
> - **Subclass 491 (Skilled Work Regional, provisional):** 5-year provisional, requires State/Territory nomination **or** eligible-family sponsorship in a designated regional area. Adds **+15** points. (Pathway to permanent residence via subclass 191.)

---

## 1. Points test breakdown

The points test is set out in the *Migration Regulations 1994* and applied identically
across 189/190/491 except for the visa-type bonus (0 / +5 / +15). Points are assessed
**at the date the invitation to apply is issued** (not at EOI submission or visa lodgement).

> **Verification status of the tables below:** the official points table page
> (`.../skilled-independent-189/points-table`, "Last updated 11/06/2026") renders its table
> via JavaScript and could not be machine-scraped. The point *values* shown are the
> long-standing legislated figures (unchanged in structure since the 16 Nov 2019 partner-points
> update) and were cross-checked against multiple migration-practitioner sources. Treat the
> exact values as **high-confidence but confirm against the live points calculator**.
> The **English test score equivalents** in §1.2 **were** scraped directly from the official
> Home Affairs English-language pages and are primary-sourced.

### 1.1 Age (at date of invitation)

| Age bracket | Points |
|---|---|
| 18–24 | 25 |
| 25–32 | 30 |
| 33–39 | 25 |
| 40–44 | 15 |
| 45–49 | 0 |

> Applicants must generally be **under 45** at invitation to be eligible for 189/190/491,
> so 45–49 effectively scores 0 and is non-viable for these subclasses.

### 1.2 English language proficiency

Points:

| Level | Points |
|---|---|
| Competent | 0 (minimum eligibility, no points) |
| Proficient | 10 |
| Superior | 20 |

> **Major change — as of 7 August 2025.** The approved English tests and their required
> scores changed. Tests taken **on/after 7 Aug 2025** use the new scores (Table A below).
> Tests taken **on/before 6 Aug 2025** remain valid for up to 3 years under the old scores
> (Table B). OET moved from alphabetical (A/B) to **numerical** scoring; PTE now has
> **per-component** minimums rather than a single number. The approved-test list expanded to
> include Cambridge C1 Advanced, CELPIP General, LanguageCert Academic and Michigan English Test (MET).
> Source: Home Affairs Superior/Proficient/Competent English pages (accessed 2026-06-28).

**Table A — scores for tests taken on/after 7 August 2025** (minimum in *each* of the 4 components: Listening / Reading / Writing / Speaking)

| Test | Competent (0 pts) | Proficient (10 pts) | Superior (20 pts) |
|---|---|---|---|
| IELTS (Academic or General) | 6 / 6 / 6 / 6 | 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 | 8 / 8 / 8 / 8 |
| PTE Academic | 47 / 48 / 51 / 54 | 58 / 59 / 69 / 76 | 69 / 70 / 85 / 88 |
| OET (numerical) | 290 / 310 / 290 / 330 | 350 / 360 / 380 / 360 | 390 / 400 / 420 / 400 |
| TOEFL iBT | 16 / 16 / 19 / 19 | 22 / 22 / 26 / 24 | 26 / 27 / 30 / 28 |
| Cambridge C1 Advanced | 163 / 163 / 170 / 179 | 175 / 179 / 193 / 194 | 186 / 190 / 210 / 208 |
| CELPIP General | 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 | 9 / 8 / 10 / 8 | 10 / 10 / 12 / 10 |
| LanguageCert Academic | 57 / 60 / 64 / 70 | 67 / 71 / 78 / 82 | 80 / 83 / 89 / 89 |
| Michigan English Test (MET) | 56 / 55 / 57 / 48 | 61 / 63 / 74 / 59 | (see Home Affairs — Superior MET not listed in scrape) |

> TOEFL iBT: you **must** select "Taking TOEFL for Australia" when registering, or the result
> is not accepted. OET is intended for health professionals only.

**Table B — scores for tests taken on/before 6 August 2025** (still valid up to 3 years; the familiar "old" thresholds)

| Test | Competent | Proficient | Superior |
|---|---|---|---|
| IELTS (each component) | 6 | 7 | 8 |
| PTE Academic (each component) | 50 | 65 | 79 |
| OET (each component) | B | B | A |

> **Passport exemption:** citizens holding a valid passport from the **UK, USA, Canada,
> New Zealand or Republic of Ireland** are taken to have **Competent** English without a test
> (but must still sit a test to claim Proficient/Superior points).

### 1.3 Skilled employment experience (last 10 years)

Two separate categories. **Combined total is capped at 20 points.** Employment must be in
the nominated (or closely related) skilled occupation, post-skill-level.

**Overseas (outside Australia) skilled employment**

| Years in last 10 | Points |
|---|---|
| < 3 years | 0 |
| 3–4 years | 5 |
| 5–7 years | 10 |
| 8–10 years | 15 |

**Australian skilled employment**

| Years in last 10 | Points |
|---|---|
| < 1 year | 0 |
| 1–2 years | 5 |
| 3–4 years | 10 |
| 5–7 years | 15 |
| 8–10 years | 20 |

> Overseas + Australian experience points may be combined but the **sum cannot exceed 20**.

### 1.4 Educational qualifications

Claim the **single highest** applicable qualification only.

| Qualification | Points |
|---|---|
| Doctorate (PhD) from an Australian institution, or other recognised PhD of equivalent standard | 20 |
| At least a Bachelor degree (includes Bachelor and Masters) from an Australian institution or recognised equivalent | 15 |
| Diploma or trade qualification completed in Australia | 10 |
| Qualification/award recognised by the relevant assessing authority as suitable for the nominated occupation | 10 |

### 1.5 Australian study requirement

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Met the Australian study requirement (≥1 CRICOS-registered course, ≥2 academic years / 92 weeks, taught in English, while physically in Australia) | 5 |

### 1.6 Specialist education qualification (STEM)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Masters by **research** or a **Doctorate** from an Australian institution including ≥2 academic years in a relevant STEM field (science, technology, engineering, mathematics, or specified ICT) | 10 |

### 1.7 Study in regional Australia

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Met the Australian study requirement while living and studying in a **designated regional area** | 5 |

### 1.8 Professional Year (PY)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Completed a Professional Year in Australia (accounting, engineering, or computer science/IT), ≥12 months, in the 4 years before invitation | 5 |

### 1.9 Accredited / credentialled community language (NAATI)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| NAATI credential at paraprofessional level or above (e.g. CCL test, or interpreter/translator credential) | 5 |

### 1.10 Partner / spouse (and single applicant) points

Claim **one** of the following:

| Situation | Points |
|---|---|
| You have **no** spouse/de facto partner, **OR** your partner is an Australian citizen or permanent resident | 10 |
| Partner is a "skilled partner": under 45, has at least **Competent** English, and a **suitable skills assessment** for an occupation on the same relevant occupation list | 10 |
| Partner has at least **Competent** English (but does not meet the skilled-partner test) | 5 |
| Partner does not meet either of the above | 0 |

> Practical note: a **single** applicant scores the same **10** points as one with a fully
> skilled partner. This is the post-16-Nov-2019 partner-points structure.

### 1.11 Visa-type / nomination bonus

| Visa | Bonus | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 189 | 0 | none |
| 190 | +5 | State/Territory nomination |
| 491 | +15 | State/Territory nomination **or** eligible-family sponsorship in a designated regional area |

### 1.12 Worked maximum (illustrative)

Maximum theoretical totals are well above any real cut-off because several categories are
mutually exclusive (you can't max age *and* 10 years' experience, etc.). The threshold that
matters operationally is the **65-point minimum to be in the pool** plus the far higher
**competitive cut-offs** (see §2).

## 2. Minimum threshold and competitive cut-offs

- **Minimum to submit an EOI / be invited:** **65 points** (confirmed on the official EOI
  page, "Last updated 14/01/2025"). Meeting 65 does **not** guarantee an invitation.
- **Reality:** for subclass 189 the effective cut-offs are far higher and occupation-specific.

**Indicative competitive ranges (as of mid-2026) — UNVERIFIED, from migration-practitioner aggregators, not the primary SkillSelect feed:**

| Occupation group | Realistic 189 floor |
|---|---|
| High-priority trades (carpenter, electrician, plumber, etc.) | 65–75 |
| Healthcare & teaching | ~80 |
| Engineering & most professional roles | 85–90 |
| ICT, accounting and other heavily-subscribed groups | 95–110+ |

- **190 and 491** typically clear at lower raw scores than 189 because of the +5 / +15 bonus
  and because State/Territory nomination criteria (not just points) gate the invitation.
  State nomination streams have their own occupation lists, residency/commitment and sometimes
  income requirements that vary by jurisdiction.

## 3. EOI / SkillSelect mechanics

Source: Home Affairs *Expression of Interest* page (Last updated 14/01/2025) and *Invitation
rounds* / *SkillSelect* pages (Invitation rounds page Last updated 23/06/2026), accessed 2026-06-28.

### 3.1 The pool and submitting an EOI

- You must submit an **EOI through SkillSelect** before you can be invited to apply for 189/190/491.
- Steps: start the EOI and save personal details → add English test results and skills
  assessment as received → submit the completed EOI → (if selected) receive an invitation to apply.
- On completion, SkillSelect shows an **indicative points score** from the information entered.
- Before submitting, the applicant should confirm: (a) their occupation is on the relevant
  **skilled occupation list**, and (b) they meet/exceed the **65-point** threshold.

### 3.2 How invitations are ranked

- Invitations are issued in **invitation rounds**.
- Ranking is by **points score (highest first)**; ties are broken by the **date and time the
  EOI reached that score** (earlier first).
- **Occupation ceilings** cap how many invitations a given occupation can receive per program
  year, applied to subclass **189** and **491 (Family-Sponsored)**, to balance the intake
  across industries. This is why a high score in an over-subscribed occupation may still wait.
- State/Territory nomination (190/491) adds a nomination step controlled by the jurisdiction,
  not purely by points.

### 3.3 EOI validity, updates, withdrawals, resubmission

- An EOI **stays in the pool for up to 2 years** from submission (commonly cited validity;
  **confirm against SkillSelect** as this was not directly scrapable here — flagged **UNVERIFIED** as to the exact period).
- You can **update** your EOI at any time **before** receiving an invitation; updates that
  change your score reset your time-priority **at the new score** as of the update.
- An EOI can be **withdrawn** and a fresh one submitted; resubmitting generally restarts
  date/time priority.

### 3.4 After an invitation

- If invited, you have **60 days from the date of the invitation** to complete and lodge the
  visa application online (confirmed on the official EOI page).
- You then upload supporting documents and lodge through the digital visa-application platform.
- If you don't apply within 60 days the invitation lapses; you may receive a second invitation,
  after which the EOI is typically removed from the pool if still not actioned.

### 3.5 Invitation rounds — frequency and recent results

- **Frequency:** historically monthly; under the current program the Department has moved to
  **larger, less frequent (roughly quarterly) rounds** aligned to annual planning levels.
- The **official** *Invitation rounds* and *Previous rounds* pages publish per-round results,
  but render the data via JavaScript and **could not be scraped** in this research session —
  figures below are from migration-practitioner aggregators and are **UNVERIFIED** against the
  primary SkillSelect feed.

| Round (subclass 189) | Invitations issued | Minimum points (range across occupations) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 Aug 2025 | ~6,887 | 65 (trades) up to ~90 (ICT/general) | UNVERIFIED |
| 13 Nov 2025 | ~10,000 | 65 (trades) up to ~90–110 (ICT/general) | UNVERIFIED |
| 4 Jun 2026 | ~10,000 | 65–100 depending on occupation | UNVERIFIED |

## 4. Recent and upcoming changes

### 4.1 Already in effect (2024–2026)

- **English tests overhaul — 7 August 2025** (confirmed, primary-sourced). New/expanded
  approved-test list, OET numerical scoring, per-component PTE minimums, and revised score
  tables. Tests taken on/before 6 Aug 2025 remain valid up to 3 years. See §1.2.
- **Migration Strategy (released 11 December 2023)** reset the policy direction: smaller,
  more targeted skilled intake; new occupation-list framework (Core Skills Occupation List);
  Skills in Demand visa replacing the TSS 482; and a commitment to **review and reform the
  points test**.

### 4.2 Proposed / under consultation (NOT yet legislated — treat as forward-looking)

The Department published a **"Review of the points test" discussion paper**. Commonly reported
proposals (all **UNVERIFIED / not legislated** as of 2026-06-28):

- **Heavier weighting toward younger applicants** (longer working/tax life).
- **Income-based points** — points for earning above a salary threshold (the Specialist Skills
  Income Threshold is frequently cited, ~$141,210, indexed), rewarding people already employed
  in higher-paid roles.
- **Possible increase of the minimum threshold from 65 to ~70 points.**
- **Implementation timing is unconfirmed** — secondary sources speculate anywhere from
  July 2026 to ~March 2027. No formal commencement date had been legislated as of this research.
